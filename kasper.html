Club History

Liverpool Football Club, widely known as Liverpool or the Reds, ranks among the most decorated sides in English and European football. Established in 1892, the club has won 20 league championships, 8 FA Cups, and 6 European Cups, with legendary players including Kenny Dalglish, Steven Gerrard, Ian Rush, and Mohamed Salah. Its famous red strip and home ground, Anfield, demonstrate a proud and lasting football heritage.

Liverpool Football Club was established on June 3, 1892, after a disagreement over Anfield prompted Everton to move away from the ground. The newly formed side took to the pitch for its first match in September 1892, then secured its first league championship in 1901, an early milestone in a remarkable story of achievement. Bill Shankly's appointment as manager in 1959 helped shape Liverpool into one of England's foremost clubs.

As football entered a new era, Liverpool built further on its traditions of resilience and achievement. The teams led by Bill Shankly and Bob Paisley collected domestic honours, and in 1977 the club lifted its first European Cup. Icons such as Kenny Dalglish, Ian Rush, and Steven Gerrard went on to leave an enduring mark on the club. In 2015, Jurgen Klopp took charge and guided Liverpool to a sixth European Cup in 2019, followed by its first Premier League crown in 2020.

Liverpool has remained a major contender in English football since 2020, lifting the League Cup in 2022 and 2024 before earning a record-equalling 20th league title in 2025. Key figures during this period have included Mohamed Salah, Virgil van Dijk, Alisson Becker, and Trent Alexander-Arnold. With Arne Slot at the helm, the club claimed the Premier League in his debut season, underlining its ongoing ambition and quality.
